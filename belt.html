10 Bench Shop / engineering: belt drive (speed ratio from pulley diameters)

Match the pulleys.

The shaft centers stay 18 in apart. Change either diameter and read the speed ratio. Set the driver to a shop rpm and the driven rpm follows. An open belt turns both pulleys the same way. A crossed belt turns the driven pulley the other way.

What a belt drive is for

Same belt, two speeds

A belt drive is two pulleys linked by a belt. The driver sets the belt speed. The driven pulley takes that speed. Ideal speed ratio is the driven diameter divided by the driver diameter.

The driven pulley is larger, so it turns slower. The driver turns 2.00 times while the driven pulley turns once.

The belt is open, so both pulleys turn the same way.

The shaft centers stay 18 in apart. An open belt that fits these pulleys is 55.1 in long. A shorter belt runs tight and heats the belt and the bearings. A longer belt slips in the groove, glazes, and heats up as it slips.

Count turns and you get the same ratio. Divide the driver turns by the driven turns. On this bench the belt does not slip, stretch, or waste speed in friction, so both pulleys share one belt speed and the ratio stays ideal.

Try this

Select Reducer. The driver is 4.00 in and the driven pulley is 8.00 in. Predict the speed ratio before you look: 8 ÷ 4 = 2.00. At 1750 rpm the driven pulley should read 875 rpm. Select 1:1. Both diameters are 6.00 in, the ratio is 1.00, and both read 1750 rpm. Select Overdrive. The driver is 8.00 in and the driven pulley is 4.00 in, so the ratio is 0.50 and the driven pulley reads 3500 rpm. Then choose Crossed belt. The ratio and the speeds stay put. The driven pulley turns the other way.

10 Shop floor Shop / engineering: belt drive (speed ratio from pulley diameters)

Diametersets the speed.

Use this page next to two pulleys and a belt, or next to a blower and its motor. The classroom use is Shop / engineering: belt drive (speed ratio from pulley diameters).

10.1

Learning objectives

  • Compute the ideal speed ratio as driven diameter divided by driver diameter.
  • Compute the driven rpm as driver rpm times driver diameter divided by driven diameter.
  • State that an open belt keeps the same rotation sense and a crossed belt reverses the driven pulley.
  • Explain that a larger driven pulley turns slower because both pulleys share one belt speed.

10.2

Classroom loop

  1. Cover the readout. Set Reducer, 4.00 in driving 8.00 in. Students compute 8 ÷ 4 and the driven rpm at 1750 rpm.
  2. Uncover 2.00 : 1 and 875 rpm. Reset the turns and watch the driver finish two turns while the driven pulley finishes one.
  3. Select Overdrive. Students predict 0.50 : 1 and 3500 rpm before the readout is uncovered.
  4. Choose Crossed belt and leave the diameters alone. The ratio stays 0.50. The driven pulley turns the other way.

10.3

On the job

An HVAC technician changes a blower belt and matches the motor pulley to the blower pulley. The motor is the driver. The blower wheel’s pulley is the driven pulley, and those two diameters set how fast the blower turns. A farm mechanic, or an agricultural equipment mechanic, reads a power-take-off belt or a combine belt drive the same way. The pulley on the power take-off and the pulley on the driven shaft tell how fast the machine will turn. A millwright aligns the sheaves on a conveyor or a fan so the belt stays in the grooves, then checks those same diameters before the machine goes back into service.

The diameter ratio sets the speed because a belt that is not slipping runs at one speed on both pulleys. A larger pulley needs more belt for each turn, so it turns fewer times in the same minute. Divide the driven diameter by the driver diameter and you have the speed ratio. That is also how many times the driver turns while the driven pulley turns once. A pulley of the wrong diameter makes the blower, the conveyor, or the combine run too fast or too slow. A belt of the wrong length is a different fault. A belt that is too short rides tight, and the heat shows up in the belt and the bearings. A belt that is too long slips in the groove, glazes, and overheats as it slips. In the shop you match the pulley sizes and the belt length together.
