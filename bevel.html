12 Bench Shop / engineering: bevel gears (turn the shaft 90°)

Turn the corner.

Miter is 1 : 1, equal teeth, shafts still at 90°. Reducer puts more teeth on the driven gear, so the output slows. Overdrive puts fewer teeth there, so the output speeds up.

What a bevel pair is for

Speed, then the corner

The driver has 20 teeth and the driven gear has 20. Speed ratio N driven / N driver is 20 / 20 = 1.00 : 1.

The driven shaft sits at a right angle to the driver. That is the 90° turn.

The teeth stay in mesh as the shafts turn. This bench does not add friction, and it does not open a gap for the teeth to jump. The speed comes only from the tooth counts.

Try this

Select Miter. Both counts are 20, the ratio is 1.00 : 1, and the driven shaft is still at 90° to the driver. Watch the brass marks take a turn together. Select Reducer and cover the readout. Compute 40 / 16, then look: 2.50 : 1, and the driven mark falls behind. Select Overdrive. The ratio drops below 1 and the driven shaft turns faster. The corner stays 90° on every preset.

12 Shop floor Shop / engineering: bevel gears (turn the shaft 90°)

At thecorner.

Use this page next to a ring and pinion, a right-angle gearbox, or the bevel set in a drill. The classroom use is Shop / engineering: bevel gears (turn the shaft 90°).

12.1

Learning objectives

  • Compute speed ratio as N driven / N driver, the same rule as spur gears.
  • State that a ratio above 1 slows the output and a ratio below 1 speeds it up.
  • Explain that the driven shaft sits at 90° to the driver.
  • Explain that bevel gears are used when two shafts must meet at a corner rather than run parallel.

12.2

Classroom loop

  1. Cover the readout. Set Miter. Students name both tooth counts and predict the ratio.
  2. Uncover 1.00 : 1 and the 90° turn. Trace the driver shaft, then the driven shaft.
  3. Set Reducer. Students compute 40 / 16 before looking. Uncover 2.50 : 1 and watch the driven mark lag.
  4. Set Overdrive. Students say the output is faster, and say why a bevel is the pair when the shafts have to turn a corner.

12.3

On the job

A differential and driveline technician works on the bevel pair in a vehicle axle, the pinion and the ring gear. The pinion is on the driveshaft. The ring gear turns the axle, and that axle sits at a right angle to the pinion. Spur gears cannot make that corner, because spur shafts have to stay parallel. A bevel pair can, because the teeth are cut on cones that meet at the corner. Tooth count sets the speed. The speed ratio is the ring’s tooth count divided by the pinion’s tooth count, N driven / N driver. A ring with more teeth than the pinion turns slower than the driveshaft and sends more torque out to the wheels. The technician reads those two counts to know the axle ratio.

A millwright lining up a right-angle gearbox on a conveyor or a mixer uses a bevel pair when the motor shaft and the driven shaft have to meet at a corner. The motor may lie along the frame while the conveyor pulley, the screw, or the mixer shaft stands at 90° to it. A spur gear cannot turn that corner. The bevel can, because the two shafts intersect instead of running side by side. Tooth count still sets the speed, the same rule as any gear pair: N driven / N driver. More teeth on the driven gear slow the conveyor or the mixer relative to the motor. Fewer teeth there, and the output runs faster.

A power-tool technician finds a small bevel set in a drill or a grinder. The motor shaft runs the length of the body, and the chuck or the wheel has to spin on a shaft at 90° to that motor. That is the reason for a bevel: the two shafts meet at a corner, and parallel gears cannot do it. Tooth count sets how fast the chuck turns relative to the motor. The speed ratio is the driven tooth count divided by the driver tooth count. A larger gear on the spindle slows the chuck. A smaller gear on the spindle is an overdrive, and the wheel or the bit turns faster than the motor. The technician counts the teeth when a worn pair is replaced, because a new gear with the wrong count changes the speed even though the corner is still 90°.
