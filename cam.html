09 Bench Shop / engineering: cam and follower (lift from rotation)

Turn the cam.

The cam speed stays steady while it plays. Move the angle to read the lift at one place on the profile. Eccentric is a smooth rise and return. Dwell holds, rises, holds, and returns. Peak is a pointed lift.

What a cam is for

One turn, one lift

A plate cam turns at a steady speed. The follower rides the profile and moves in a straight line. Read the cam angle, then read the follower lift. One full turn of the cam is one lift cycle.

A dwell is a stretch of the turn where the lift stays put. A rise is the climb. A return is the way back down. The eccentric has no dwell. It rises through the first half of the turn and returns through the second. The dwell cam holds low, rises, holds high, and returns. The peak cam stays low, then lifts and returns on a short nose.

Eccentric cam, 120°, follower lift 0.56 in. This part of the profile is the rise, so the follower is climbing.

This model is kinematic. It leaves out spring force, friction, and bounce. The follower simply rides the profile. Change the speed and the lift at a given angle stays the same. The profile is what sets the lift.

Try this

Select Eccentric and set the angle to 180°. The lift reads 0.80 in, the high point. Switch to Dwell at that same angle: 1.00 in, and the status is high dwell. Switch to Peak: 1.00 in, at the nose. Now set the angle to 120° and leave the speed alone. Eccentric reads 0.56 in and is still in the rise. Dwell reads 0.20 in, early in a steeper rise. Peak reads 0.00 in and is still on the low dwell. On the dwell cam, 130° is the middle of the rise, so the lift is 0.50 in. The high dwell holds 1.00 in from 160° to 280°.

Profilesets the lift.

09.1

Learning objectives

  • State that one full turn of the cam produces one lift cycle.
  • Read follower lift at a given cam angle.
  • Name rise, dwell, and return on the displacement diagram.
  • Explain that the profile sets the lift at each angle. Cam speed leaves that lift alone.

09.2

Classroom loop

  1. Select Eccentric. Cover the lift. Students predict a smooth climb from 0.00 in at 0° to 0.80 in at 180°, then a smooth return. No dwell.
  2. Uncover. Scrub from 0° to 180° and back to 0°. The status changes from rise to return at the high point.
  3. Select Dwell. At 40° the lift is 0.00 in, a low dwell. At 130° it is 0.50 in, the middle of the rise. At 200° it holds 1.00 in, a high dwell. At 310° it is 0.50 in again, the middle of the return.
  4. Select Peak and keep the speed. At 120° the follower is still down. At 180° the lift is 1.00 in. The pointed profile packs the lift into a short part of the turn.

09.3

On the job

An automotive technician, or an engine builder, reads a camshaft lobe to see how far a valve will lift and when in the turn that lift happens. A packaging-machine technician times a cam that opens a gate so a box or a product moves at the right moment in the cycle. A sewing-machine technician, or a textile technician, adjusts a cam that drives a needle or a feed so the motion lands on the part of the turn the stitch needs.

The shape of the profile sets how hard the lift is, and when it happens. Cam speed sets how fast the turn goes by. A smooth eccentric lobe spreads the rise and the return across the whole turn, so the follower climbs and falls gently. A dwell-rise-dwell-return profile holds the follower still, then lifts it, holds it up, and brings it back. A pointed lobe puts the lift into a short piece of the turn, so the follower moves faster and the change is sharper even when the cam speed stays the same. One full turn of the cam is still one lift cycle.
