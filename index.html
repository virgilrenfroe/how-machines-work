Shop / engineering: gear ratio & meshing

Gear ratio& meshing.

A gear train passes rotation from one tooth to the next. Gear ratio is the driven tooth count divided by the driver tooth count. A larger driven gear turns slower and with more torque. An idler changes direction and does not change the ratio.

02 Bench Shop / engineering: gear ratio & meshing

Count the teeth.

Same module on every gear, so a larger count is a larger pitch circle. Only the end gears set the ratio.

03 · What to notice

Teeth in mesh

Each mesh is external: a tooth enters a space and the next gear turns the other way. Pitch circles stay tangent. The small gap is backlash so the teeth do not bind.

The idler reverses direction and cancels out of the ratio. Driver and driven turn the same way. Gear ratio still uses only N driven / N driver.

Try this

Pause on Reducer. Predict the driven turn count after the driver mark goes around once (0.40). Reset turns, play, and check the counter. Then change only the idler. The ratio should sit still. Turn the idler off and watch the driven mark reverse.

04 Shop floor Ten minutes at the bench

Ten minutesat the bench.

Use this page as the model next to a real pinion and gear. The classroom use is gear ratio and meshing, not a rendering study.

04.1

Learning objectives

  • Compute gear ratio as N driven ÷ N driver.
  • Predict output speed and whether torque rises or falls.
  • Explain that equal module is what lets unlike gears mesh.
  • State that an idler changes direction and not the ratio.

04.2

Classroom loop

  1. Hand out the Reducer tooth counts. Students compute 40 ÷ 16 before looking at the readout.
  2. Uncover 2.50 : 1. Ask how far the driven gear turns after one driver turn.
  3. Run the train. Stop near one driver turn and compare the counter.
  4. Move only the idler. Ratio stays. Then remove the idler and name the new direction.

04.3

Shop examples

Lathe change gears, a drill-press reduction, a clock train. This exhibit is spur gears in external mesh. It leaves out friction, shaft windup, and chain drives such as a bicycle.

04.4

On the job

A machinist sets the change gears on a lathe so the lead screw moves the tool the right distance for each turn of the spindle. The ratio is still the driven tooth count divided by the driver tooth count. A millwright bolts a gear reducer onto a conveyor: a small pinion drives a larger gear, the output shaft turns slower, and the torque at the belt goes up.

An idler in that train only flips the direction of the next shaft. It does not change how many times the output turns for one turn of the motor. Count the two end gears. Leave the idler out of the ratio.

04.5

Common mistakes

  • An idler changes the gear ratio. It changes direction. The ratio still uses only the driver and the driven.
  • Gear ratio is driver teeth divided by driven teeth. On this bench the ratio is N driven ÷ N driver. Reducer 40 ÷ 16 is 2.50 : 1.
  • Gears must be the same size to mesh. They need the same tooth size. The tooth counts can differ.
  • More teeth on the driver always means more torque out. A larger driver lowers the ratio and speeds the output up.
